Hi, I’m Carolyn.

I work in international markets, helping businesses grow beyond their home borders. Over the years, I’ve built things from the ground up, worked through plenty of challenges, and developed my own experience and judgment along the way.

But if you asked me what has helped me do this work well, I think part of the answer would lie in another interest of mine: detective fiction.

The more mysteries I read, the more I notice how much they have in common with my work. You often see the outcome before you understand the cause. A number suddenly changes. Something that was going smoothly stalls. A decision you felt confident about fails to deliver the result you expected.

That’s where the interesting part begins.

You go back to the scene, look for the details you missed, and work out what’s a coincidence and what’s a clue. You form a theory, then find evidence that challenges it. Sometimes, the first explanation is the easiest to believe, but it isn’t necessarily the closest to the truth.

That’s why I often think of myself as a detective at work. I tend to ask one more question: Why did this happen? What evidence supports this explanation? Are we addressing the problem itself, or just one of its symptoms?

Following those questions has helped me get things done. Over time, it has also given me a way of making sense of problems and finding answers. That’s what my work has taught me, and it gives me a foundation whenever I encounter something new.

Then AI came along.

For those of us working in international business and cross-border commerce, this change is hard to ignore. Capabilities that once took considerable time and practice to develop are becoming accessible in new ways. Familiar tasks can suddenly be approached differently. It’s exciting, but it also raises some questions: Where does our hard-earned experience fit now? How much of what we know how to do should we learn to do again?

I began learning about AI with those questions—and my own work experience—in mind.

What interests me most is putting it to work on real problems I understand. Having done the work myself, I know where the difficulties tend to lie. That also gives me something concrete to ask: Does this answer hold up? How far can it help me get? Where do experience and judgment still need to come in?

As I learn and experiment, I’m coming to see how experience and new technology can strengthen each other. Experience helps me know what to ask and whether an answer is useful. AI gives me a chance to look at a problem from another angle and explore paths I might not have considered—or had the time to pursue.

That process has sparked a fresh sense of curiosity in me.

It feels a little like reading a good mystery. Just when you think you understand the rules of the story, a new clue changes the picture. You have to look again and rethink your theory. That’s exactly what makes you want to keep turning the pages.

This blog grew out of wanting to document that process.

Here, I share what I’ve learned from working in international markets: how things get started from scratch, how I look for causes when something goes wrong, and the detours I only understood in hindsight. I also write about learning and using AI: why I try a particular approach, whether it actually helps, and what I’m still trying to figure out.

Reading belongs here, too. A novel might draw my attention to a detail I would otherwise have missed. A book from an entirely different field might help me understand a work problem that has puzzled me for months. I want to write about those connections, because some things only become clear when different parts of our experience begin to meet.

If you also work in international business, I hope some of these experiences help you avoid a few unnecessary detours. If you’re starting to explore AI but aren’t sure how it fits into your own work, I hope my experiments give you a concrete place to begin. And if, like me, you simply enjoy reading and asking why things happen, you’re very welcome here.

I’m still learning, and I’ll keep revising my views as I go. What I hope to share is experience you can follow from the original question through the attempts to answer it—and perhaps try out for yourself.

Because finding a clue is satisfying. If sharing it helps someone else take their next step, that makes it all the more worth writing down.
